# Engine and cooling system

## Normal operating temperatures
Normal coolant temperature during work is about 80–100°C. Readings above 105°C mean the machine
should be checked at the next opportunity (maintenance due). Above 110°C the engine is overheating:
reduce load straight away and follow the ENGINE_OVERHEAT procedure in *fault-codes.md*.

## Why engines overheat on site
The most common causes, in order:
1. **Blocked radiator core** from dust, leaves or mud, especially in demolition and dusty mining work.
2. **Low coolant** from a leaking hose, clamp or water pump seal.
3. **Slipping or broken fan belt.**
4. **High ambient temperature combined with heavy continuous load**, for example pushing uphill in
   summer.
5. **Faulty thermostat** stuck closed.

## Cooling down safely
Do not shut off a hot engine immediately after heavy work. Run it at low idle for 3–5 minutes so the
turbocharger and cylinder head cool evenly. Never open the radiator cap on a hot engine: pressurised
coolant can cause severe burns. Wait until coolant is below 50°C, then open the cap slowly.

## Cleaning the radiator
With the engine off and cool, blow compressed air (below 2 bar) or low-pressure water through the core
from the fan side outwards. Check the fins are straight. In very dusty conditions, clean the core
daily. A reversing fan, if fitted, can be run at the start of each shift to clear debris.

## Engine warm-up
In cold weather, let the engine run at low idle for about 5 minutes before applying load. Do not run
the engine at high RPM when cold. Cycle the hydraulic functions slowly to warm the hydraulic oil.
