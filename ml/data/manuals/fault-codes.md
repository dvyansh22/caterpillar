# Fault code troubleshooting guide

Each fault code below lists what it means, what the operator should do immediately, and what the
technician checks. The component named in each section is the part highlighted in AR field repair.

## HYD_PRESSURE_LOW — low hydraulic pressure
**Component:** hydraulic pump and main relief valve.
**Meaning:** average hydraulic pressure has dropped below about 180 bar (normal working range is
roughly 220–300 bar). Implements feel slow or weak, and the boom may drift down under load.
**Operator action:**
1. Lower all implements to the ground and move the machine to a safe, level spot.
2. Check the hydraulic oil level in the sight glass with the machine level and oil warm.
3. Look for fresh oil under the machine, around hose fittings and cylinder rod seals.
4. If oil is low or a leak is visible, stop work and report the fault. Do not keep operating.
**Technician checks:** hydraulic oil level and grade, suction strainer, burst or chafed hoses,
pump output pressure at the test port, main relief valve setting, and cylinder seal bypass.

## ENGINE_OVERHEAT — engine coolant over temperature
**Component:** radiator and cooling fan.
**Meaning:** coolant temperature has gone above 110°C. Continued operation can warp the cylinder
head or damage the head gasket.
**Operator action:**
1. Reduce load immediately and let the engine run at low idle for 3–5 minutes to cool down.
   Do not shut the engine off straight away unless there is steam, a coolant leak or an alarm.
2. After shutdown, never open the radiator cap while the engine is hot. Wait until the coolant
   is below 50°C.
3. Check the coolant level in the expansion tank and look for debris blocking the radiator core.
**Technician checks:** coolant level and mixture, radiator core blockage (clean with low-pressure
air from the fan side), fan belt tension, fan drive, thermostat, water pump and the temperature
sender. See also *engine-cooling-system.md*.

## AIR_FILTER_RESTRICTED — air filter restricted
**Component:** engine air filter (primary and secondary elements).
**Meaning:** the intake restriction indicator has tripped. The engine is breathing through a
clogged filter, which reduces power and raises fuel use and exhaust smoke.
**Operator action:** finish the current cycle, park, and report it. In dusty sites, check the
pre-cleaner bowl and empty it.
**Technician checks:** replace the primary element. Replace the secondary (safety) element every
third primary change or if it is dirty. Never clean a filter by tapping it on the machine, and never
run the engine without a filter fitted. Reset the restriction indicator after service.

## FUEL_FILTER_CLOGGED — fuel filter clogged
**Component:** primary fuel filter / water separator and secondary fuel filter.
**Meaning:** fuel flow is restricted. Symptoms are loss of power under load, hesitation, or the
engine stalling. Contaminated fuel is the usual cause.
**Operator action:** drain water from the water separator bowl if trained to do so, then report the
fault. Avoid running the tank near empty, because it pulls sediment into the filters.
**Technician checks:** replace both fuel filters, prime the fuel system, inspect the tank for water
and sediment, and check the fuel supply for contamination.

## TRACK_TENSION — track tension out of range
**Component:** track adjuster (grease cylinder) and idler.
**Meaning:** track sag is outside the allowed range. A loose track can come off the idler; a tight
track wears pins, bushings and sprockets quickly.
**Operator action:** stop travelling, park on level ground, and report it. Do not attempt to adjust
the tension yourself unless trained: the adjuster holds grease at very high pressure.
**Technician checks:** measure sag midway between the carrier roller and the idler (about 20–30 mm
for most small and medium excavators and dozers), clean packed mud from the undercarriage, and add or
release grease through the adjuster valve.

## BRAKE_WEAR — brake wear limit reached
**Component:** service brake discs / wet brake packs.
**Meaning:** brake wear has reached the service limit. Stopping distances increase, especially on
downgrades and when loaded.
**Operator action:** reduce speed, keep extra distance on haul roads, use retarder and lower gears on
downgrades, and report it. If braking feels weak, stop the machine in a safe place and do not continue.
**Technician checks:** measure brake wear with the wear indicator, check brake oil level and
temperature, inspect for oil contamination, and test the service, secondary and parking brakes.

## TIRE_PRESSURE_LOW — tire pressure low
**Component:** tire and wheel assembly.
**Meaning:** one or more tires are below the recommended cold inflation pressure. Low pressure causes
overheating, sidewall damage and, on large mining tires, a risk of tire explosion.
**Operator action:** reduce speed and report it. Never approach the tread face of a hot or damaged
large tire; approach from the side. Do not weld or heat near an inflated tire.
**Technician checks:** check cold pressure with a calibrated gauge, inspect for cuts and bulges,
check the valve and rim, and inflate with nitrogen where site rules require it.
