# Haul truck operation

## Haul road speed
Follow the posted haul-road speed limits. The general limit for loaded off-highway trucks on this site
is 50 km/h on straight, dry roads, and lower on ramps, bends, intersections and wet or dusty roads.
Exceeding the limit is recorded as unsafe operation. Loaded trucks need much longer to stop; allow at
least three truck lengths behind the truck ahead.

## Descending ramps
Select the gear and retarder speed before starting down the ramp, not on it. Use the retarder to hold
speed and keep the service brakes for stopping. If the retarder or brakes overheat, stop in a safe
spot and let them cool. A BRAKE_WEAR fault means stopping distances are longer: slow down and report it.

## Loading at the shovel
Approach the loading unit only when signalled. Stop square to the shovel, apply the brake, and stay in
the cab with the seatbelt fastened during loading. Watch for the operator's horn signals: one to
stop, two to move off (check your site's signal rules).

## Dumping
Dump only on level ground, at least one truck width from the edge unless a safety berm is present.
Raise the body only when the truck is stopped with the brake applied. Lower the body fully before
moving off; driving with the body raised can hit overhead lines or roll the truck.

## Blind spots and light vehicles
Large haul trucks have blind spots that can hide a pickup truck completely. Light vehicles must have
positive radio contact before approaching a haul truck. Use the proximity alerts and cameras, and sound
the horn before moving off (one blast before forward, two before reverse).

## Fatigue on long shifts
Haul driving is repetitive, which makes fatigue a major risk on 10–12 hour shifts. Take the scheduled
breaks, drink water, and report high fatigue scores immediately. See *safety-procedures.md*.
