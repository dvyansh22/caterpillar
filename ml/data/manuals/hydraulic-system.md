# Hydraulic system

## How it works
The engine drives a hydraulic pump that pushes oil at high pressure (typically 220–300 bar while
working) to control valves and then to cylinders and motors. The operator's joysticks move the control
valves. Oil returns through a filter and a cooler to the tank.

## Checking the hydraulic oil level
Park on level ground, lower all implements to the ground in the position shown on the tank decal
(for excavators: stick vertical, bucket curled, on the ground), shut down, and read the sight glass.
The level should be between the marks. Add only the oil grade shown on the tank.

## Signs of a hydraulic problem
- Slow or weak implement movement, especially when the oil is hot.
- Boom or blade drifting down while the joystick is in neutral.
- Whining or screeching from the pump (often low oil or air entering the suction line).
- Oil temperature warning or foamy oil in the sight glass.
- Fresh oil on hoses, fittings or cylinder rods.

## Hose and leak safety
Never use your hand to search for leaks. A pinhole leak at working pressure can inject oil through
the skin, which is a medical emergency. Use a piece of cardboard to locate leaks. Relieve pressure
(engine off, cycle the joysticks with the key ON) before loosening any fitting.

## Hydraulic filter and oil service
Replace the hydraulic return filter at the first 250 hours and then every 500 hours. Replace hydraulic
oil every 2,000 hours or as oil analysis recommends. Take an oil sample at every 500-hour service.
